SOC-213 Semester Project

📖 Family Book Project

Build one 12-slide presentation across the semester. Complete one family-analysis slide during each content module, then finish the separate Key Takeaways, Final Reflection, and References slides in Module Eight.

Project purpose

The Family Book connects personal or observed family experiences to larger sociological patterns. Choose your own family or a family you know well enough to analyze responsibly. Protect privacy by changing names and omitting identifying details. This is an analytical project, not a request for private disclosure or a scrapbook.

Required 12-slide sequence

  1. Slide 1: Cover with the project title and your name.
  2. Slides 2–9: Eight module slides, one completed during each module.
  3. Slide 10: Key Takeaways, with two takeaways from each section.
  4. Slide 11: Final Reflection, completed during Module Eight.
  5. Slide 12: References, with complete APA-style entries.

Include one meaningful image on every module slide and on the Final Reflection slide. Add useful alt text and source information or an AI-generated-image label.

What every module slide must do

  • Answer the module-specific prompt.
  • Use a concrete, privacy-protected family example.
  • Apply at least two relevant course concepts rather than listing definitions.
  • Connect the example to an institution, culture, inequality, policy, historical change, or social process.
  • Use student-authored wording and cite borrowed ideas, evidence, quotations, and images.

Semester workflow and template

Download the PowerPoint template once. Keep the prompts while you plan, then replace the guide text with your own work. Add the assigned slide during each module, update the References slide as you use sources, save the same file, and maintain a backup.

AI use

Permitted: asking AI to explain a course concept, checking grammar after you write, or creating an image when appropriate.

Not permitted: asking AI to write, rewrite, paraphrase, expand, or summarize slide content for submission. The analysis and wording must be your own. Verify AI-generated images for bias or inaccuracies and label them as AI-generated.

Privacy and accessibility

Use pseudonyms and omit identifying details. Do not disclose abuse, diagnoses, legal histories, immigration status, or other sensitive information unless doing so is essential, voluntary, and safe. You may select a different example. Images need useful alt text, readable contrast, and a source. Contact the instructor or Disability Services if the format creates an access barrier.

Module slide guide

Module One: Introduce the selected family and explain how its structure, roles, culture, or history illustrates family as a social institution.

Module Two: Turn a family observation into a researchable question, identify a suitable method, and explain which theory could interpret the evidence.

Module Three: Analyze one gender message, role, or expectation and explain how family, school, media, or another institution reinforced or challenged it.

Module Four: Explain how work, income, wealth, social class, inflation, caregiving, or public policy shaped the selected family.

Module Five: Analyze a relationship pattern using partner selection, love, cohabitation, marriage, communication, or life-course concepts.

Module Six: Analyze family formation, parenting, attachment, development, modeling, ecology, or epigenetics.

Module Seven: Explain how a privacy-protected family challenge affected roles, resources, coping, or support and identify a factor that could reduce harm or strengthen resilience.

Module Eight: Analyze a transition such as divorce, remarriage, stepfamily formation, aging, caregiving, retirement, loss, or another later-life change.

Slides completed in Module Eight

Slide 10 Key Takeaways: record two concise takeaways from every section and explain why each idea matters.

Slide 11 Final Reflection: explain how your sociological understanding of family changed, identify the ideas that most influenced your thinking, connect family guidance and preparation to modern society, and describe how one insight may influence your future decisions.

Slide 12 References: include the coursebook and every source used for ideas, statistics, quotations, videos, and outside images. Alphabetize entries and use hanging indents when possible.

Final quality check

  • All 12 slides are present and in order.
  • Every module slide answers its prompt and applies at least two concepts.
  • The Key Takeaways and Final Reflection are separate slides.
  • Every required image has alt text and attribution.
  • Brief citations agree with complete entries on the References slide.
  • Names and sensitive identifying details are protected.
